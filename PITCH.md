# Fair Play FPS — the pitch

> **Valve took 128-tick away. The community's been asking for it back. We're giving it to them — with an anti-cheat built in from day one.**

## What this is

A new competitive FPS built around the feel and mechanics of Counter-Strike, from 1.6 through CS2, with Condition Zero and Source in the mix. This isn't a clone. It's a new experiment built from pieces of all those games and engines, with fair play as the foundation everything else sits on.

## Valve built a casino and forgot the game

Valve turned Counter-Strike into a gambling site and a bank — skins, their own currency, the whole psychological hook machine — and forgot the mechanics. The game feels smooth and triple-A, sure, but underneath that the mechanics are gone: things were forgotten about or left half-finished under "works in progress," and their anti-cheat is still miles from perfect. People don't stick around because the game got better. They stick around because of sunk cost and the money locked in their skins. Fair Play FPS answers all three at once: mechanics first, a custom anti-cheat built from the inside out as the core instead of bolted on, and no greed. The game serves the players, not a casino.

## Fair play is the core, not a patch

The anti-cheat isn't Valve's, and it isn't bolted on after the fact. It's built from the inside out, as the thing the whole game is designed around. Certain ledgers are zero-touch. Scripts are allowed only to a limited extent. Servers are plugin-based, with hard restrictions around the anti-cheat so the community can build without ever weakening it.

## Tick rate: earn it, don't fake it

We ship at 64-tick with subtick-style optimization, so it feels like 128. Then we climb the tick rate as the anti-cheat and the optimization mature. The game trains and evolves around its players and its community, instead of promising everything on day one.

## Community-driven and open source

Skins and guns are community-based. Servers are plugin-based. This is built for hardcore CS players, the people who put thousands of hours in, not for newcomers. Open source, owned by the people who play it.

## Linux-only, on purpose

The target is Linux: CachyOS and other distros, through Steam on Linux. No Windows support. That choice is what keeps the game honest about the kernel it runs on, and it's a home the big studios gave up on.

## Where it comes from

Counter-Strike: Source is the bought study reference. The lineage also runs through the Source mod scene that people loved: Garry's Mod, Deathrun, surfing, zombie mods. And through CS:GO preservation, like the `csgo_legacy` beta branch, for studying the original feel.

## Priority and scope

Fair Play FPS comes first, then the other existing projects in order. No new games, no new game ideas. Everything is built from the foundations that already exist.

Live dev sessions at night, when the connection is best. The build process out in the open: writing it, testing it, and how I actually talk to my software.

# Competitive Shooter Lineage Matrix

Status: Research and prototype guide
Owner: Joan Pujols

## Method

Each era is evaluated through four decisions:

- **Keep** — a durable principle worth preserving
- **Reject** — a weakness or obsolete constraint
- **Modernize** — a good idea needing current technology or accessibility
- **Test** — a player-memory or technical hypothesis requiring evidence

This is design research. It does not authorize copying code, assets, maps,
sounds, names, characters, branding, or protected layouts.

## Counter-Strike 1.5 and 1.6

### Keep

- Immediate input and crisp movement response
- Strong acceleration, stopping, counter-strafing, and jump skill
- Low-clutter geometry and fast target recognition
- Stable map callouts and durable spatial memory
- Clear weapon roles and high mastery ceiling
- Round economy, loss tension, and team consequences
- LAN, community servers, demos, and offline practice

### Reject

- Dependence on undocumented quirks as the only path to mastery
- Weak onboarding and accessibility
- Inconsistent community-server integrity
- Historical account, distribution, and update fragmentation
- Visual settings that can create unfair visibility differences

### Modernize

- Preserve movement depth with explicit documented curves
- Preserve readable geometry with modern materials and lighting
- Make demo and replay evidence deterministic and searchable
- Keep community hosting while separating ranked authority
- Make legal objective, plant, collision, and penetration states visible

### Test

- Stop distance at representative strafe speeds
- Jump, landing, crouch, ladder, and air-control timing
- Weapon recovery and movement-inaccuracy perception
- Target recognition under multiple quality and accessibility settings
- LAN feel at fixed server tick and high client frame rate

## Counter-Strike: Condition Zero

### Keep

- GoldSrc competitive feel with refreshed presentation
- Tour of Duty as structured offline learning
- Named bots with skill, reaction, teamwork, and weapon profiles
- Navigation meshes and bot-supported practice
- Familiar maps presented with stronger visual landmarks
- Separation of competitive rounds from the Deleted Scenes campaign

### Reject

- Treating scripted campaign and competitive play as one design problem
- Bots that see or react through invalid information
- Progression requirements that teach narrow exploits instead of broad skill
- Packaging ambiguity caused by its multi-studio development history

### Modernize

- Use bots as transparent practice partners and controlled security red team
- Build player-selected drills from authoritative replay evidence
- Give bots explainable senses, reaction time, uncertainty, and team intent
- Make offline practice share the exact server rules used online

### Test

- Joan's current Condition Zero movement and weapon memories
- Which bot behaviors feel useful, unfair, predictable, or humanlike
- Which updated maps preserve classic callouts versus add clutter
- Whether Tour of Duty creates transferable competitive skill

## Counter-Strike: Source

### Keep

- Modern material, lighting, sound, animation, and tool ambitions
- Dedicated server and Source SDK community
- Physics as tactical feedback where it remains deterministic and readable
- The bot lineage carried forward from Turtle Rock's Condition Zero work

### Reject

- Decorative physics objects that obstruct movement or sight unpredictably
- Softer or less legible feedback when authoritative outcomes occur
- Map detail that damages silhouette recognition
- Ragdoll or prop differences that affect competitive information

### Modernize

- Separate competitive collision from cosmetic physics
- Replicate only gameplay-relevant physical state authoritatively
- Use modern rendering without hiding opponents in dark corners
- Expose latency, interpolation, and correction diagnostics to development

### Test

- Movement and stopping against the 1.6 and Condition Zero baseline
- Physics-prop effect on routes, grenades, visibility, and frame pacing
- Hitbox, animation, and hit-feedback agreement
- Community-server practices worth preserving

## Counter-Strike: Global Offensive

### Keep

- Competitive reconciliation between classic expectations and Source technology
- Matchmaking, ranks, team economy, utility depth, and spectator clarity
- Long-term map and lineup mastery
- Broad hardware support and esports readability
- Global Elite as meaningful evidence of accumulated skill

### Reject

- Cheating and smurfing systems that make honest players distrust outcomes
- Opaque trust, enforcement, rank, and appeal behavior
- Account and inventory value becoming inseparable from competitive identity
- Engagement or marketplace incentives overriding match quality

### Modernize

- Explainable rank confidence and smurf detection
- Native replay evidence and reason categories
- Fair new-player, returning-player, household, and private-practice paths
- Separate cosmetic ownership from competitive authority

### Test

- Joan's remembered Global Elite skill loop and strongest motivation
- Which weapon and movement changes improved or weakened classic feel
- Rank confidence under inactivity, account recovery, and platform migration
- Skilled-player false positives in fair-play scoring

## Counter-Strike 2

### Keep

- Source 2 rendering and content-pipeline ambition
- Responsive environmental smoke as legible shared state
- Continuous platform evolution rather than a disposable sequel
- Modern diagnostics, accessibility, and broad display support as goals

### Reject

- Technical claims that do not match player-perceived response
- Breaking trusted muscle memory without measurable benefit
- Visual richness that hides enemies or destabilizes frame time
- Silent live changes that prevent players from understanding outcomes

### Modernize

- Publish measured input-to-result and network behavior for each build
- Version movement, weapon, map, and detector policy
- Retain previous known-good simulation profiles for controlled comparison
- Treat player feel reports as data requiring replay and telemetry correlation

### Test

- Sub-tick event ordering versus fixed authoritative simulation
- Smoke, shadow, and player visibility at competitive settings
- Frame-time spikes and their effect on input and hit perception
- How updates affect veteran muscle memory and new-player comprehension

## Fair Play FPS synthesis

### Keep

- Classic clarity, mastery, economy, and map memory
- Condition Zero's offline practice and bot profiles
- Source-era tools and presentation where they preserve readability
- CS:GO's competitive depth and spectator language
- CS2's modern platform ambition

### Reject

- Client authority
- Unexplained outcomes
- Accidental clutter and darkness advantages
- Opaque punishment
- Smurfing treated as one simple identity problem
- Marketplace systems before the game earns trust

### Modernize

- Linux-first authoritative simulation
- LAN baseline plus regional dedicated servers
- Predictive clients with bounded reconciliation and lag compensation
- Deterministic evidence and replay
- Native fair-play validators and controlled adaptive research
- Original visual identity, maps, weapons, audio, and branding

### First tests

1. Graybox two-room LAN arena, corridor, close corner, and objective zone
2. Walk, strafe, stop, jump, crouch, collision, and one original rifle
3. Fixed server tick and two real clients
4. Dark-corner visibility matrix
5. Latency, jitter, loss, and reconciliation laboratory
6. Deterministic replay and shot evidence viewer
7. Controlled movement and fire-cadence attacker bots

## Joan's current memory anchors

- More than thirteen years across multiple Counter-Strike eras
- Play before Steam became the universal account path
- Strong experience with 1.6, Condition Zero, CS:GO, and CS2
- Global Elite as an important competitive achievement
- Train as a favorite map
- Dark corners, tunnels, repeated hiding spots, and durable callout memory
- High-impact sidearm timing and the punishment of missing
- Frustration with cheating, smurfing, lost account history, and changing feel
- Playing the reference games while directing the new game in real time

These notes are product evidence. Specific values still require direct capture,
